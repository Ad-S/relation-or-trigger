# Relation or Trigger? Findings

All numbers are from `RESULTS.md` (generated by `analyze.py`). The figures are in `figures/`.

## Setup actually used, and how it differs from the proposal

- **Frozen encoders, no fine-tuning.** We used the proposal's fallback: the encoder is frozen and the
  classifier is a linear readout trained per fold. The previous project showed that a linear probe on frozen
  DeBERTa-v3-large-NLI is as good as the fine-tuned model. Motte-and-bailey AUC was 0.718 for the frozen
  probe against 0.674 fine-tuned; strawman was 0.736 against 0.770. Every claim below is therefore about
  what the frozen encoder makes linearly available and causally routes to that readout.
- **Encoders.** DeBERTa-v3-large with the MNLI/FEVER/ANLI/LingNLI/WANLI checkpoint ("NLI"),
  DeBERTa-v3-large without NLI training ("plain"), and the same architecture with random weights
  ("random"). There is also a B-only input to the NLI encoder.
- **Data.** The 683 claim pairs from the previous project. The 416 minimal pairs are new: for each of
  the 208 originals there is one counterfactual edit (cf: the label flips and the trigger is kept word for
  word) and one trigger-swap edit (ts: the trigger is removed, or added if the original had none, and the
  label is kept). The minimal pairs are used only for analysis, never as part of a test score.
- **Evaluation protocol.** Grouped 5-fold CV by conversation, the same grouping as the earlier
  fine-tuning. Every pair is evaluated with probes and readouts fitted without its conversation.

## RQ1: Where can the label be linearly decoded?

- **Motte-and-bailey.** On B's tokens the label is at chance at layer 0 (AUC 0.50) and rises to 0.73 by
  layers 21–24 (NLI). Shuffled-label controls stay around 0.50, and random-init stays at chance.
  With B-only input, the same tokens plateau at about 0.60–0.64. **The extra decodability needs A and C
  in the input**, and it appears in the last third of the network.
- **Strawman.** B's tokens are already at 0.68 at layer 0 and reach 0.79 at layer 22. The B-only input
  is about as good (0.76 at its best), so **for strawman most of the signal is in B's own wording**.
- **The A tokens alone give 0.60–0.67 at every layer, including layer 0.** This is a topic or dataset
  cue in the A wording, not reasoning, and should be reported as a confound.

## RQ2: Relational or lexical? Are the two separable?

- **The trigger is decoded almost perfectly from layer 0** (AUC about 0.95 on B). From layer 4 it is
  equally decodable from the rest of B with the trigger tokens excluded. It reaches A and [CLS] at
  about 0.6–0.8.
- **The trigger and fallacy directions are nearly orthogonal.** Their cosine is 0.04–0.12 for
  motte-and-bailey and −0.02 to −0.08 for strawman, at every layer.
- **The minimal pairs separate relation from trigger** (Exp 2, all tokens, NLI, layer 24). Shifts along
  the fallacy direction are in held-out SD units; "cf" means the shift toward the new label after a
  counterfactual edit, and "ts" means the absolute shift after a trigger swap.

  | Task | cf shift | ts shift |
  |---|---|---|
  | Strawman | +1.30 | 0.32 |
  | Motte-and-bailey | +0.41 (peak 0.73 at layer 20) | 0.37 |

  The cf and ts curves are equal at layer 0 and separate from about layer 14. Random-init shows no cf
  shift. Bootstrap CIs are in `RESULTS.md`.
- **Erasure** (Exp 4). The full numbers are in `RESULTS.md`.
  - *Amnesic probing.* Removing the trigger direction with LEACE changes label AUC by at most ±0.01 at
    every layer; a random direction gives the same result.
  - *Erasure inside the forward pass.* Erasing the trigger at any layer and reading out at layer 24
    gives the same picture: NLI motte-and-bailey stays between 0.644 and 0.661 against a baseline of
    0.655.
  - *Entailment.* Erasing the entailment direction costs a little, about 0.02–0.06, mostly late. In
    the forward pass the largest drop is strawman at layer 24, 0.713 → 0.663.
  - *Caveat.* LEACE removes only the linear, rank-1 trigger information. The trigger stays decodable
    elsewhere in B.

**Answer.** The readout is not driven by the trigger phrase, for either task. For strawman the evidence
is strong: counterfactual edits move the readout about 4× more than trigger swaps. For motte-and-bailey
it is weaker: counterfactual edits move it 2–3× more in layers 20–24 than trigger swaps, but the
absolute effects are small and the readout itself is weak (AUC about 0.65). The earlier
trigger-masking result, where motte-and-bailey accuracy dropped when the phrase was deleted, came from a
fine-tuned model. It may reflect that model, or the crude deletion making the text ungrammatical; the
frozen readout here does not show it.

## RQ3: Which layers, positions and components matter causally?

These results come from residual-stream patching (Exp 3) with the layer-24 readout.

- **Size of the effect.** The readout gap between the original and its edit is much larger for
  counterfactual edits than for trigger swaps.

  | Gap (NLI) | Motte-and-bailey | Strawman |
  |---|---|---|
  | Counterfactual edit | 1.02 SD | 1.36 SD |
  | Trigger swap | 0.35 SD | 0.32 SD |
  | Trigger-swap pairs with a gap ≥ 0.5 SD | 22–30% | 22–30% |

  This is the behavioural version of the RQ2 result.
- **Where the effect lives.** Up to about layer 16 the effect sits entirely in B's positions: B
  recovery is 0.86–1.0, and context ([CLS] + A + C) recovery is under 0.05. After that it moves into
  the context positions. At layer 23, context recovery is 0.46 for motte-and-bailey and 0.33 for
  strawman, and **most of that is carried by the A tokens**. **The comparison of B against A is written
  into A late, in layers 17–23.**
  - *Caveat.* The readout mean-pools all tokens, A included, so late patching at A partly acts on the
    readout directly. Read this as "information has moved into A", not as a full circuit.
- **Components.** Attribution patching was verified with real patching, and the two agree; the
  rank-ordering is the same.
  - The components that write the B-dependent signal into context positions are **MLPs in layers
    21–23** and a few **layer 20–23 heads**. For motte-and-bailey these are L21/L22 MLP (each about
    0.22 recovery) and heads L23H0, H8, H13. For strawman they are L23H11, L23H5, L20H3, L21H2 and
    L22/L23 MLP.
  - Mean-ablating the top-10 heads on held-out real rows:

    | Task (NLI) | Baseline AUC | Top-10 heads ablated | 10 random heads, same layers |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Strawman | 0.713 | 0.443 | 0.690 |
    | Motte-and-bailey | 0.655 | 0.607 | 0.579 |

    For strawman this is a large, specific effect. For motte-and-bailey the random draw hurt more,
    so it is a null result.
  - For the plain encoder all ablation effects are small, at most 0.03.

## RQ4: Does NLI pretraining get reused?

- **Behaviourally, partly yes.** The NLI encoder separates the minimal pairs more than the plain one:
  - Counterfactual shift at layer 24, NLI vs plain: 1.30 vs 0.92 SD for strawman, and a 0.73 vs 0.50
    SD peak for motte-and-bailey.
  - Patching gap for counterfactual pairs, NLI vs plain: 1.36 vs 1.04 SD for strawman, and 1.02 vs
    0.71 SD for motte-and-bailey.
  - Late-layer heads matter causally only in the NLI encoder (strawman: 0.71 → 0.44 vs 0.63 → 0.60).
  - Entailment erasure hurts the NLI readout more at layer 24.
  - Label AUC barely differs between the two encoders on B's tokens.
- **At the level of directions, no evidence.** The cosine between the fallacy direction and the
  entailment direction is small and negative (about −0.03 to −0.20), the same in NLI, plain and
  random-init. It just reflects how the two labels correlate in the data (r = −0.12 for
  motte-and-bailey and −0.24 for strawman), not a shared direction.

## Limitations to state in the report

- **Sample size and edits.** There are about 100 originals per task. The minimal-pair edits were written
  with LLM assistance and checked automatically, not yet by hand; `data/minimal_pairs_review.csv` holds 30 per
  type to check. Only large effects should be treated as findings.
- **Frozen encoder plus linear readout, not a fine-tuned classifier.** For motte-and-bailey the readout
  is weak (AUC 0.64–0.66), so its patching denominators are noisy.
- **Narrowing is an NLI proxy** (P(A⇒B) − P(B⇒A)), not hand annotation, and is barely decodable before
  layer 24.
- **The head-ablation control** uses one random draw of heads, and the top heads were chosen using all
  counterfactual pairs, so the selection overlaps the test conversations. Mean ablation leaves the
  token positions unchanged.
- **Not done** (optional in the proposal): steering, integrated gradients, MDL probing, B-to-A vs
  B-to-trigger attention statistics, the fine-tuned-model extension, and inter-annotator agreement.
